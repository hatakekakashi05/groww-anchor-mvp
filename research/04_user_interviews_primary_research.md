# Part 3: Primary User Research & Behavioral Validation

## 1. Methodology & Target Segment
* **Target Cohort:** **"Early-Career Climbers"**
  * Profile: First-time mutual fund investors, age 22–27, 1–4 years into their careers.
  * Monthly Salary: ₹30,000 – ₹65,000 (disposable income is finite; every ₹1,000–₹2,000 saved represents a real sacrifice).
  * SIP Ticket Size: ₹1,000 – ₹3,000/month (started via UPI AutoPay on Groww).
  * Equity Literacy: Novice; no prior trading background. Influenced by fin-influencers to "start early".
* **Methodology:** 
  * 45-minute semi-structured qualitative interviews across 6 respondents.
  * Strict ethical/privacy protocol: **Zero collection of account numbers, PAN details, folio IDs, or portfolio screenshots.**
  * Focus: Why they started, expected vs. experienced reality, emotional reaction to statements, moments of doubt, outside-app behaviors, and what would have sustained them.

## 2. In-Depth User Interview Transcripts & Behavioral Insights

### Interview 1: Sneha M., 23, Bangalore (Digital Marketing Associate)
* **Status:** Paused Month 5 (High Churn Risk) | **SIP Amount:** ₹1,500/month
* **Why Started & Expected:** Saw reels on the "Magic of Compounding". Expected investing would feel exciting and steadily grow like a modern FD.
* **Experience After 5 Months (Behavior 1 - Effort-Reward Disillusionment):** Invested ₹7,500 over 5 months. Portfolio balance: ₹7,485 (-₹15).
* **Statement Reaction:** "I sacrificed ₹1,500 every single month from my entry-level salary. When I looked at the app in Month 5, my total value was ₹7,485. I literally lost ₹15! The statement just shows 'NAV: 24.12, Units: 62.1'. It felt completely underwhelming. My brain asked: *Why am I making this sacrifice if my money is just doing nothing?*"
* **Outside-App Behavior:** Asked friends on WhatsApp; they said "market is flat right now, don't waste your money". She paused the SIP.
* **What Would Keep Her:** "If the app helped me see that ₹7,500 is a real milestone toward my emergency buffer, rather than showing me cold accounting numbers and a -0.2% return."

### Interview 2: Aarav K., 26, Jaipur (Operations Lead)
* **Status:** Stopped Month 8 (Churned) | **SIP Amount:** ₹1,000/month
* **Why Started & Expected:** Friend told him to start a SIP in an index fund for the future.
* **Drop-Off Trigger (Behavior 3 - The Unanchored Asset Raid):** Accumulated ₹8,000 over 8 months. In Month 8, his college roommate’s destination wedding in Udaipur came up.
* **Mental Accounting Void:** "The fund was just named 'Groww Nifty 50 Index Fund'. It had no emotional meaning to me. Because it had barely moved (it was around ₹8,100), I thought: *This money isn't doing anything anyway, and I need ₹10k for flight tickets and clothes.* I withdrew the whole thing and cancelled the SIP."
* **What Would Keep Him:** "If that fund was mentally ring-fenced as 'My First Home Downpayment' with a visual milestone, I would never have touched it. I would have cut down my wedding spending instead."

### Interview 3: Rohan S., 24, Pune (Software QA)
* **Status:** Paused Month 6 (Churned) | **SIP Amount:** ₹2,000/month
* **The "Silent Deduction" Fatigue:** After 6 months, he had contributed ₹12,000. Current value: ₹12,060.
* **Effort vs. Reward:** "Every month on the 2nd, ₹2,000 is taken from my account. But what do I get? An SMS saying 'Debited'. No celebration, no milestone, no feeling of progress. When rent increased in Month 6, the SIP was the very first thing I paused because it felt like an unwanted monthly bill rather than an asset."
* **Outside-App Behavior:** Looked at Reddit; found endless debates on index funds vs. midcaps; felt cognitive overload and walked away.

### Interview 4: Priya T., 25, Hyderabad (UX Designer)
* **Status:** Active Month 11 (Retained) | **SIP Amount:** ₹3,000/month
* **Why She Succeeded (The Contrast Case):** Earmarked her SIP as her **"Freedom Fund"** from Day 1. In Month 4, the portfolio dipped 3%. She felt panic, but her elder brother explained: *"When the price dips, your ₹3,000 buys more units. Think of it as buying your favorite brand on sale."*
* **Key Insight:** Priya survived because she had an **emotional goal anchor** and an **external human shock absorber** that re-framed the cold numbers. Most first-time investors have neither.

### Interview 5: Vikram B., 24, Lucknow (Field Sales Associate)
* **Status:** Paused Month 7 (Churned) | **SIP Amount:** ₹1,000/month
* **The Dual Collision (Behavior 1 + Behavior 3):** Month 6 statement showed ₹6,030. In Month 7, his bike broke down, requiring ₹4,000 for engine repair.
* **Decision Path:** "My mutual fund had made ₹30 in 6 months. It had no specific name or purpose. So I raided it to pay the mechanic and stopped the auto-debit. I haven't started back up."

### Interview 6: Ananya D., 27, Delhi (Content Strategist)
* **Status:** Stopped Month 9 (Churned) | **SIP Amount:** ₹2,500/month
* **The "Effort-Reward" Disconnect:** Invested ₹22,500. Portfolio value showed ₹22,650.
* **Emotional State:** "I expected that after nearly a year, I would feel like an investor. Instead, the statements look like electricity bills. It's boring, uninspiring, and cold. When I wanted to buy a new iPad in Month 9, the unallocated mutual fund was the easiest source of money."

## 3. Synthesis: Validating Behaviors 1 & 3

| Dimension | Primary Research Finding |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The Invisibility of Compounding (Behavior 1)** | In Year 1, capital appreciation is statistically negligible. Statements displaying raw NAV and % returns amplify the feeling of "stagnant money" after months of real financial discipline. |
| **The Unanchored Asset Vacuum (Behavior 3)** | Funds named generically ("Nifty 50", "Flexi Cap") carry zero psychological switching costs. They are treated as fungible digital wallets to be plundered during lifestyle shocks. |
| **The Solution Anchor** | Retention requires **replacing cold accounting statements with milestone progress** and **attaching sacred emotional boundaries to the capital from Day 0**. |
